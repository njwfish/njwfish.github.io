About

Statistics for biological and healthcare systems

I am a PhD student in Statistics at Harvard University, advised by Marinka Zitnik.

I develop statistical and machine learning methods for biological and healthcare systems. My research asks how causal information can be learned and transported across scales, from molecular and cellular measurements to patients and health systems.

My current work includes distribution-valued learning, single-cell perturbation modeling, transcriptomic deconvolution, and treatment-effect estimation from electronic health records. Across these settings, I aim to build methods that make their data-generating assumptions explicit and support scientific intervention, not prediction alone.

Before Harvard, I completed an MSc in Statistics at the University of Oxford as a Rhodes Scholar and studied computer science and sociology at Stanford University.
